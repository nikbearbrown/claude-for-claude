## Preface

Most people arrive at Claude through the chat interface and leave without ever learning what else is there. Claude is not a single assistant — it is an ecosystem of tools with different capabilities, different risks, and different supervisory demands. This book exists to close that gap. It teaches non-specialist professionals, students, educators, and researchers how to choose the right Claude surface — Claude AI, Claude Code, or Claude Cowork — by matching the tool to the work, the risk, and the human judgment required.

The operating discipline this book builds is not prompting. It is workflow diagnosis: look at a task, decide which surface belongs on it, specify the work clearly, constrain what the tool can touch, inspect the result, and document the decision. That loop applies whether you are drafting a research memo, running a file-heavy reporting workflow, or asking a coding agent to refactor a script. The loop also tells you when to stop — when the work is too sensitive, too consequential, or too poorly defined to delegate at all.

The book moves in three acts. Act One names the tools and teaches the distinction among Claude's three primary surfaces. Act Two builds the operating loop — specification, constraint, supervision, and the human gate. Act Three transfers that discipline to real work domains: research, writing, data analysis, education, coding support, and operations. A capstone project routes one realistic task through all three surfaces and asks the reader to decide what must remain human-owned throughout.
